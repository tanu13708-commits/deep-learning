# 08 — Attention & Transformers

## 57. What problem does attention solve?
Attention lets a model focus on the most relevant parts of an input when producing a representation or prediction. In sequences it can connect one position directly to other relevant positions instead of relying only on a fixed-size recurrent state.

## 58. What is attention mechanism?
Attention computes a weighted combination of values, where weights indicate how relevant different inputs are to the current query. The weights are derived by comparing queries with keys.

## 59. What are Query, Key, and Value?
- **Query (Q):** what the current position is looking for.
- **Key (K):** what each position offers for matching.
- **Value (V):** information that is actually aggregated.

Query-key similarity determines attention weights applied to values.

## 60. How does self-attention work?
In self-attention, Q, K and V come from the same sequence. A common formula is:

**Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dₖ)V**

Steps: create Q/K/V → calculate similarity → scale → softmax → weighted sum of V.

## 61. What is multi-head attention?
Multi-head attention performs several attention operations in parallel using different learned projections. Different heads can learn different relationships. Their outputs are concatenated and projected.

## 62. Why do Transformers need positional information?
Self-attention alone does not inherently encode sequence order. Positional information allows the model to distinguish different arrangements such as “dog bites man” and “man bites dog”. It can be added using positional encodings or learned/relative positional representations.

## 63. Transformer vs RNN?
**RNN:** processes sequence step by step, naturally carries order through recurrence, and is harder to parallelize across positions. **Transformer:** uses attention, can process positions in parallel during training, and models long-range relationships effectively. Transformers need positional information.

## 64. What is an encoder and decoder in a Transformer?
The **encoder** converts an input sequence into contextual representations. The **decoder** generates an output sequence, commonly autoregressively, using masked self-attention and, in encoder-decoder architectures, attention to encoder representations. Encoder-only and decoder-only Transformers also exist.

## 65. Why did Transformers become important for modern NLP and GenAI?
They model long-range relationships through attention, support highly parallel training, scale effectively with data and compute, and provide a strong foundation for large pretrained models. They underpin many modern language and generative AI systems.
